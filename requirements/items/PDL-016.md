# PDL-016 — Preserve decision and discovery records while protecting personal, confidential, security-sensitive, and commercially sensitive information

**Requirement ID:** PDL-016  
**Priority:** MUST  
**Source:** [product-discovery-and-delivery.md](../product-discovery-and-delivery.md) — Overview  
**Status:** Active requirement; no implementation or evaluation evidence is recorded in this repository.  
**Rule controls:** CHG-06, MEDIA-01, MEDIA-02, MEDIA-03, MEDIA-04, PDL-01, PDL-03, PDL-05  
**Acceptance checks:** None recorded  
**Open decisions:** None recorded

## 1. Normative requirement

Preserve decision and discovery records while protecting personal, confidential, security-sensitive, and commercially sensitive information.

## 2. Why this requirement exists

This requirement is part of **Overview** in [product-discovery-and-delivery.md](../product-discovery-and-delivery.md). It is traced to 8 rule control(s): CHG-06, MEDIA-01, MEDIA-02, MEDIA-03, MEDIA-04, PDL-01, PDL-03, PDL-05. Those controls state the following purpose:

- **CHG-06 — Change execution and closure runbook:** Give a contributor or agent an end-to-end, repeatable procedure to accept, investigate, design, implement, verify, review, hand off, and close work without losing scope, evidence, user changes, operational readiness, or accountability.
- **MEDIA-01 — Video production lifecycle:** Produce product demos, tutorials, and marketing videos as evidence-backed, rights-cleared, accessible, secure, and reviewable work products with clear production gates and ownership.
- **MEDIA-02 — Video publication and asset lifecycle:** Publish the approved video to the correct destinations with complete metadata and accessible alternatives, retain or remove production assets deliberately, and respond to corrections, rights changes, incidents, and product evolution.
- **MEDIA-03 — Media design system and content templates:** Provide a coherent, accessible, reusable creative system for videos and related web content, while requiring each production to choose components that fit its audience, evidence, platform, and purpose.
- **MEDIA-04 — Production code, assets, and website integration:** Treat video creation automation, large assets, and website playback as maintained product work with clear boundaries, reproducible outputs, quality gates, accessibility, privacy, and operational ownership.
- **PDL-01 — Problem discovery and evidence:** Ensure that work begins with a sufficiently supported account of the problem, affected people and systems, desired outcome, uncertainty, and decision authority. Prevent a proposed implementation from being mistaken for validated need.
- **PDL-03 — Solution alternatives and experimentation:** Select solutions through explicit, evidence-based decisions and use bounded investigations to reduce uncertainty without confusing experimental artifacts with supported product behavior.
- **PDL-05 — Acceptance, release, learning, and retirement:** Demonstrate that the delivered change meets approved outcomes, make a controlled release decision, observe actual results, and safely change or retire capabilities when evidence warrants it.

Acceptance is not satisfied by citing these controls. The implementation must demonstrate the observable result in section 6, and the mapped rule clauses in section 5 define what must hold at the enforcement boundary.

## 3. Priority and interpretation

This requirement has **MUST** priority under the repository's requirement conventions. A MUST requirement is needed for the stated product direction or a safe release. A SHOULD requirement is expected unless a documented tradeoff justifies deferral. A COULD requirement is a candidate capability that requires product or technical validation. Acceptance describes observable evidence; it does not mandate a specific implementation unless the requirement says so.

## 4. Scope and applicability

This requirement belongs to **Overview** in [product-discovery-and-delivery.md](../product-discovery-and-delivery.md). It applies to the product area named by that source section and to every implementation, interface, release artifact, document, or operation that claims to satisfy it.

The mapped controls add the following applicability boundaries:

- **CHG-06:** Applies to every feature, bug fix, security remediation, refactor, dependency/toolchain update, documentation/design change, build/release change, data migration, operational change, and rule/requirement change. Use a lightweight path for low-impact, reversible changes, while retaining applicable phases and evidence.
- **MEDIA-01:** Applies to original or substantially edited recordings, screen captures, voiceovers, animation, generated media, product demonstrations, tutorials, launch announcements, customer/user stories, and video used in documentation, the landing page, or external channels. Applies to employees, contractors, agents, and vendors. A quick internal diagnostic recording may use a shortened path if it is not published, contains no sensitive content, and has an expiry/deletion owner.
- **MEDIA-02:** Applies from final approval through upload, distribution, monitoring, revision, archival, takedown, and deletion of all media artifacts and associated records.
- **MEDIA-03:** Applies to visual and audio identity, video narratives, motion/graphic components, reusable project templates, thumbnails, captions, and video embeds for product demos, tutorials, marketing, and training. This control defines the system and selection process; actual logo, palette, typefaces, voice, and brand personality require an approved brand decision and are not invented here.
- **MEDIA-04:** Applies when video production uses scripts, source code, render frameworks, command-line tools, plugins, templates, CI, asset storage, a website player/embed, or automated publication. Also applies to manual workflows where asset and export consistency require controlled configuration.
- **PDL-01:** Applies when creating or materially changing a product capability, service, deployment profile, operational workflow, public commitment, system boundary, or remediation. For routine, low-risk maintenance, apply the lightweight path in PDL-04 while retaining source, expected outcome, risk, and checks.
- **PDL-03:** Applies to choices with material impact on architecture, security, data, supported platforms, vendors, user workflows, licenses, operational burden, cost, or public commitments. Small reversible choices may be recorded in the change description if it preserves the required decision evidence.
- **PDL-05:** Applies to product capabilities, services, APIs, data stores, model artifacts, deployment profiles, documentation sites, workflows, and public commitments from implementation acceptance through operation and end of life.

The source requirement table remains authoritative if this view and the source disagree.

## 5. Detailed obligations

Each subsection below is the complete normative content of a mapped control. A requirement is not satisfied by selecting one convenient clause; every applicable clause must hold, including its negative and boundary behavior.

### CHG-06 — Change execution and closure runbook

*Domain:* [change-management](../../torviello-enforcer/change-management/README.md) · *Rule:* [06-change-execution-and-closure-runbook.md](../../torviello-enforcer/change-management/rules/06-change-execution-and-closure-runbook.md)

**Control objective.** Give a contributor or agent an end-to-end, repeatable procedure to accept, investigate, design, implement, verify, review, hand off, and close work without losing scope, evidence, user changes, operational readiness, or accountability.

#### CHG-06.01 — Accept and classify work before acting

At intake, record the request/source, affected users/system, desired outcome, urgency, requester, accountable owner, and initial impact. Classify the work by change type, affected release/profile, data/security/privacy/safety/compatibility/reliability impact, reversibility, and urgency. Check for conflicts, duplicate work, authority, access, and dependencies. If scope or owner is unclear, record the ambiguity and perform only safe, reversible discovery until the owner resolves it. Emergency work follows the emergency-change control.

#### CHG-06.02 — Establish a clean and safe working context

Before editing, inspect repository status, current branch/revision, applicable instructions, requirements, rules, related code/tests, and existing local changes. Preserve user and collaborator changes; do not reset, overwrite, clean, or rebase them without explicit authorization. Confirm the target branch/worktree and intended files. Record the base revision and environment when they affect reproducibility. If repository state conflicts with the task, stop the conflicting operation and report the specific blocker while continuing independent read-only analysis.

#### CHG-06.03 — Discover and specify proportionately

For material work, follow the discovery and requirements rules: reproduce defects, establish evidence, affected profiles, scope/non-goals, requirement IDs, acceptance, risks, unresolved decisions, alternatives, and relevant quality constraints before implementation. For low-risk maintenance, document expected behavior, affected boundary, and checks in the change record. Do not treat an implementation request as approval for unrelated scope or as proof of the proposed solution.

#### CHG-06.04 — Plan work with owners, dependencies, and gates

Create a plan appropriate to the work size. Decompose into reviewable outcomes; identify owner roles, dependencies, sequence, uncertainty, risk, milestones, validation, documentation, migration, rollout, and rollback/forward recovery where applicable. For parallel work, name file/module ownership and integration points. Define what would block, descope, or stop the work. The plan MUST preserve durable decisions and requirements outside transient private planning notes.

#### CHG-06.05 — Implement in bounded increments

Before each increment, state its intended output and acceptance check. Follow local conventions and domain rules; keep changes within approved scope, update tests alongside behavior, and separate unrelated cleanup. Inspect the diff after each meaningful increment. If implementation reveals changed assumptions, unexpected risk, incompatible behavior, or scope growth, pause affected work, update evidence/spec/plan, and obtain the appropriate decision before proceeding.

#### CHG-06.06 — Verify the change at the affected boundaries

Select checks from the requirement acceptance criteria and technical domain controls. Run the narrowest fast checks first, then relevant integration, security, compatibility, performance, migration, accessibility, build, and release checks. Include failure/negative cases for affected trust, data, resource, reliability, and compatibility boundaries. Record exact commands/procedures, revision/profile/environment, result, skipped/unavailable checks, and limitations. Do not infer success from code inspection when behavior can be tested.

#### CHG-06.07 — Prepare an independently reviewable change

Before review, inspect the complete diff for unintended files, secrets, generated artifacts, unrelated changes, missing tests, stale comments, API/documentation mismatch, and overlooked requirement/control updates. Provide a concise description of problem, behavior, scope, design choices, alternatives where material, tests/evidence, risks, migration/release notes, and remaining questions. Ensure reviewers have access to cited artifacts and avoid requiring private plan content when policy prohibits publishing it.

#### CHG-06.08 — Review with appropriate independence and resolve findings

Assign reviewers based on touched boundaries and risk, including domain/security/operations/product/accessibility/data authorities where relevant. Authors MUST NOT self-approve a change requiring independent review. Reviewers assess correctness, simplicity, maintainability, failure behavior, access boundaries, test quality, compatibility, operations, and requirement acceptance—not style preference alone. Track each finding to resolution, documented disagreement, or authorized risk acceptance. Re-run checks after material revisions.

#### CHG-06.09 — Accept and hand off with evidence

The authorized owner accepts only when specified outcomes have evidence, required reviews/checks pass, failed or skipped checks are dispositioned, release-critical risks have authority, and documentation/operations handoff is ready. Handoff includes changed behavior, deployment/configuration/migration steps, monitoring and alert implications, recovery/rollback limits, support owner, known limitations, and user communication where applicable. Mark partial or conditional acceptance explicitly with scope, owner, conditions, expiry, and prohibited use.

#### CHG-06.10 — Release or deploy through the owning gate

Do not equate code merge with release. Follow release and operations controls for artifact integrity, approvals, staged exposure, health/readiness, stop conditions, rollback, and post-deploy checks. Capture release identity and environment. If a gate fails, stop or roll back according to the approved plan; record incident/change evidence and do not suppress a failure to report success.

#### CHG-06.11 — Close only after durable records and follow-up ownership exist

At closure, update requirement dispositions, decision records, docs/design/API/canvas, tests, changelog/release artifacts, runbooks, monitoring, and deprecation records affected by the work. Record actual outcome, verification, release state, known limitations, unresolved risks, and links to evidence. Assign owner and due/review trigger to follow-ups; do not leave risk in an unowned comment. Retain only records permitted by policy and remove temporary credentials/data safely.

#### CHG-06.12 — Learn from defects, rework, and variance

When work misses acceptance, causes a regression/incident, materially exceeds estimates, or repeats review findings, identify the cause and whether a requirement, design, test, rule, tooling, or handoff failed. Create corrective action with owner and effectiveness check; update relevant torviello-enforcer/runbooks when the procedure itself was insufficient. Avoid blame-based conclusions that prevent accurate reporting.

### MEDIA-01 — Video production lifecycle

*Domain:* [media-production](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/README.md) · *Rule:* [01-video-production-lifecycle.md](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/rules/01-video-production-lifecycle.md)

**Control objective.** Produce product demos, tutorials, and marketing videos as evidence-backed, rights-cleared, accessible, secure, and reviewable work products with clear production gates and ownership.

#### MEDIA-01.01 — Approve a production brief before production

The producer MUST record the video’s purpose, intended audience, user need, distribution context, desired action/outcome, owner, approver, length/format constraints, deadline, budget/resources, success measure, and planned review/expiry. Identify whether the material is a product demo, tutorial, training, launch, or promotional claim because these types require different evidence and review. Do not begin costly or externally visible production from an unapproved idea alone.

#### MEDIA-01.02 — Establish truth and scope for every product claim

For each factual claim about features, performance, security, compatibility, availability, pricing, licensing, or roadmap, record an authoritative source, tested version/profile, measurement conditions where applicable, and approval owner. Demonstrations MUST show the real product state for the stated version or be clearly labeled as mockup, prototype, edited composite, or planned capability. Do not fabricate user outcomes, benchmark results, testimonials, endorsements, or availability. Remove or qualify claims that cannot be substantiated at publication time.

#### MEDIA-01.03 — Plan narrative and capture before recording

Prepare a script, outline, or storyboard proportionate to complexity. Specify scenes, narration/on-screen text, required product state and test data, transitions, call to action, accessibility needs, expected duration, and owner for each factual segment. Rehearse demonstrations on a controlled profile using non-sensitive data. Capture a clean take and note deviations or edits that alter meaning. For tutorials, include prerequisites and recovery steps, not just the success path.

#### MEDIA-01.04 — Clear rights and permissions before using material

Before recording or editing, inventory music, fonts, images, footage, logos, product/customer names, voices, likenesses, testimonials, code, model output, and third-party interfaces. Record creator/source, license/permission, attribution, permitted channels/territories/term, restrictions, and evidence. Obtain required releases and approvals for identifiable people, customer content, or confidential environments. Do not assume publicly viewable content is reusable. Escalate unclear ownership, commercial usage, privacy, or jurisdiction issues to authorized review before publication.

#### MEDIA-01.05 — Protect secrets, personal data, and production systems during capture

Use a dedicated demo account/environment with least privilege, synthetic data, test credentials, and representative but non-sensitive content. Before and after capture inspect the full screen, notifications, browser history, terminal output, logs, environment variables, file paths, access tokens, customer data, and background audio for accidental disclosure. Revoke any credential exposed in raw footage, even if later cropped or blurred. Do not connect recording workflows to production systems unless explicitly approved and risk-controlled.

#### MEDIA-01.06 — Edit for accuracy, comprehension, and reproducibility

Edits MUST preserve factual context and MUST NOT imply behavior, causality, speed, quality, or availability that the source did not demonstrate. Keep the project file and source provenance sufficient for another authorized editor to reproduce the final render. Record significant edits, compositing, speed changes, AI-generated or synthetic media, and removed limitations where those could affect viewer interpretation. Maintain a versioned review copy and a separate approved release render.

#### MEDIA-01.07 — Make published speech and meaningful sound accessible

Provide synchronized captions for speech and meaningful audio, including speaker identification and relevant sound cues. Review captions for accuracy, timing, names, technical terms, and punctuation; automatic captions alone are not accepted without human review. Provide a transcript for long or instructional media where useful, and audio description or equivalent narration when essential visual information is not conveyed in audio. Ensure text overlays remain readable, high contrast, sufficiently long, and do not communicate meaning only through color or motion. Apply WCAG 2.2 media criteria and the target publishing surface’s accessible player requirements.

#### MEDIA-01.08 — Review the complete video as a release artifact

Before publication, an independent reviewer MUST check the full beginning-to-end rendered video, not only the script or a partial preview. Review claim accuracy, product/version fidelity, rights and releases, captions/transcript, audio clarity, text legibility, pacing, spelling, accessible playback, privacy/security leakage, visual defects, links/call-to-action, and consistency with the approved brief. For performance or security claims, require evidence from the relevant domain owner. Record findings and resolved/dispositioned exceptions.

#### MEDIA-01.09 — Maintain reproducible technical outputs

The production owner MUST define source project format, render settings, aspect ratio, frame rate, resolution, audio level/format, caption format, naming/version scheme, and destination-specific deliverables before final render. Preserve a high-quality archival master where retention is approved, then produce platform-specific encodes from that master. Validate the rendered file’s duration, audio/video synchronization, captions, resolution, playback, and file integrity. Platform-specific settings are confirmed against current official destination guidance at each release.

#### MEDIA-01.10 — Keep work reviewable and separate from engine runtime

Store scripts, project files, source assets, licenses, renders, and production automation under the approved media ownership boundary. Media tooling and assets MUST NOT become build or runtime dependencies of the inference engine unless a separate requirement and architecture decision explicitly establish that dependency. Use version control for text/scripts/configuration; use approved object storage or asset management for large binaries, with stable references, access control, retention, backup, and checksums as appropriate. Do not commit secrets or restricted raw footage.

### MEDIA-02 — Video publication and asset lifecycle

*Domain:* [media-production](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/README.md) · *Rule:* [02-video-publication-and-asset-lifecycle.md](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/rules/02-video-publication-and-asset-lifecycle.md)

**Control objective.** Publish the approved video to the correct destinations with complete metadata and accessible alternatives, retain or remove production assets deliberately, and respond to corrections, rights changes, incidents, and product evolution.

#### MEDIA-02.01 — Publish only the approved release package

Publication MUST use the release render and sidecar assets (captions, transcript, thumbnail, description, attribution, links) tied to the approved revision. Verify destination account, audience/visibility, title, description, privacy settings, monetization if applicable, captions, links, and publication date before confirmation. Record destination URL/ID, version, date, publisher, owner, and approval. Do not upload raw review copies or source footage as public content.

#### MEDIA-02.02 — Keep metadata accurate and maintain accessible alternatives

Descriptions, chapters, labels, thumbnails, captions, transcripts, and linked documentation MUST accurately represent the content and product state. Avoid misleading thumbnail/title claims. Provide an accessible transcript or equivalent content path when needed for the material’s purpose, and ensure the destination player supports keyboard and assistive technology use. Captions MUST remain available after edits or re-encoding.

#### MEDIA-02.03 — Track distribution, versions, and dependents

Maintain an inventory of each published asset, its version, destination(s), intended audience, owner, claims-evidence links, rights terms/expiry, source project, dependent pages/docs/campaigns, review date, and support contact. Coordinate updates across destinations; do not assume replacing one copy updates embeds, downloads, or third-party reposts.

#### MEDIA-02.04 — Monitor feedback and detect content defects

The owner MUST review viewer/user reports, broken links, caption defects, privacy disclosures, rights complaints, playback/encoding issues, and product inaccuracies. Define a response route and severity-based response expectation. Analytics MUST follow the project’s privacy and telemetry rules, collect only approved signals, restrict access, and honor retention choices. Viewer counts alone do not demonstrate that the intended learning or product outcome was achieved.

#### MEDIA-02.05 — Correct or withdraw materially inaccurate or unsafe material

When claims become false, a secret or personal data disclosure is found, rights are disputed, a vulnerability is exposed, or the video can cause material user harm, the owner MUST assess promptly whether to unlist, restrict, replace, correct, or remove the affected content. Follow security/privacy/incident/legal escalation controls, preserve evidence as authorized, and coordinate affected embeds and stakeholders. Do not leave misleading content public solely to preserve campaign metrics.

#### MEDIA-02.06 — Revalidate content when the product or claim changes

Set review triggers for product version, UI/API changes, compatibility claims, pricing/licensing policy, benchmark methodology, security posture, destination policy, and rights expiry. Revalidate the affected scenes and linked claims before continued promotion. Mark superseded versions clearly or retire them; do not present stale instructions as current.

#### MEDIA-02.07 — Govern source files and raw asset retention

Classify scripts, raw recordings, project files, releases, credentials, and final renders. Store them in approved locations with role-based access and backup appropriate to business need. Define retention by purpose, rights, privacy, and legal obligations; remove unnecessary takes and sensitive data after the approved retention period. Keep enough licensed source material and project metadata to reproduce or responsibly update the final output, unless rights or privacy restrictions require deletion.

#### MEDIA-02.08 — Retire and delete media with dependency closure

When a video is retired, identify public destinations, embeds, downloaded copies under project control, dependent documentation/campaigns, rights records, source files, and user links. Provide a replacement or redirect where needed, remove/restrict controlled copies, update links and inventories, revoke access to expired private assets, and record what cannot be recalled from third parties. Preserve only records needed for audit, legal, or provenance purposes under the applicable retention policy.

### MEDIA-03 — Media design system and content templates

*Domain:* [media-production](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/README.md) · *Rule:* [03-media-design-system-and-content-templates.md](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/rules/03-media-design-system-and-content-templates.md)

**Control objective.** Provide a coherent, accessible, reusable creative system for videos and related web content, while requiring each production to choose components that fit its audience, evidence, platform, and purpose.

#### MEDIA-03.01 — Maintain an approved media style guide

The product/design owner MUST maintain a versioned media style guide with approved logo usage, color tokens, typography and fallback fonts, grid/margins, title-safe areas, contrast rules, icon/illustration treatment, motion principles, transition rules, audio identity, caption conventions, thumbnail layout, and examples of correct/incorrect usage. Record the style-guide owner, version, supported languages, and review trigger. Where brand decisions are unresolved, use neutral accessible defaults and label them provisional; do not fabricate official branding.

#### MEDIA-03.02 — Define reusable production components and their contracts

The media system MUST define reusable components or templates with purpose, inputs, variants, duration/placement expectations, typography/color tokens, animation behavior, sound behavior, accessibility alternatives, editable source, and allowed customization. The inventory MUST consider, as applicable: opening/title card; logo bug; lower third/name and role; section/chapter marker; product UI frame/device mockup; cursor/interaction highlight; callout/annotation; comparison/before-after panel; diagram/architecture frame; metric/benchmark card; quote/testimonial card with permission; code/terminal panel; warning/limitation card; end card/call-to-action; thumbnail; captions; and audio bed/stinger. Components MUST be omitted when they add no informational value.

#### MEDIA-03.03 — Choose narrative structure by viewer task

Select and document a structure appropriate to the content: problem → outcome → evidence for a short product overview; prerequisites → steps → verification → troubleshooting for a tutorial; context → architecture → flow → failure/recovery for a technical walkthrough; claim → method → result → limitations for a benchmark; or an explicitly reasoned alternative. Define the opening promise, information sequence, proof, transitions, ending, and call to action before production. Do not force every video into one marketing format.

#### MEDIA-03.04 — Specify shot and screen-capture grammar

The script/storyboard MUST identify the capture type per scene (screen capture, presenter, narration over graphics, animation, live action, or mixed), composition/framing, cursor/focus behavior, text legibility, product state, scene duration target, and transition intent. For screen recordings, set a stable viewport/scale, remove distracting UI and notifications, use predictable cursor movement, zoom only to support comprehension, and use prepared synthetic data. Preserve sufficient context so viewers can understand where an action occurs. Avoid rapid cuts or motion that prevents reading or causes discomfort.

#### MEDIA-03.05 — Use visual hierarchy and motion to clarify information

Each scene MUST have a primary message and hierarchy among headline, visual evidence, annotation, and supporting detail. Keep text within the chosen destination’s safe area and readable at expected mobile display size. Motion MUST explain sequence, focus, state, or relationship; use restrained duration/easing and provide a reduced-motion or static equivalent for essential web experiences. Do not use flashing content, color alone, tiny captions, or animation as the only carrier of meaning. Validate overlays against the actual footage and final encoded output.

#### MEDIA-03.06 — Define audio and voice treatment

Specify narration language, pronunciation of product terms, recording environment, microphone distance, noise treatment, music/sound-effect role, voice/music priority, channel layout, loudness target, peak ceiling, and export profile. Dialogue MUST remain intelligible on common speakers/headphones and in mono fold-down where applicable. Music and effects MUST not mask speech or captions. Measure the final render against the selected delivery target; do not adopt broadcast loudness targets blindly for every online platform. Retain clean dialogue/music stems when rights and storage policy allow future remixes.

#### MEDIA-03.07 — Provide content-type templates, not one universal video

Maintain distinct reusable briefs/storyboard templates for product overview, feature demo, step-by-step tutorial, architecture/engineering explainer, benchmark/performance report, launch announcement, and short-form cutdown when those formats are in scope. Each template MUST ask for the claims/evidence, user prerequisite, version/profile, story sequence, visual components, accessibility deliverables, length/platform constraints, and approval roles relevant to that format. A template MAY be omitted by an owner decision when that format is not in scope.

#### MEDIA-03.08 — Design for destination and aspect-ratio adaptation

Choose a primary master aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, color space, and audio layout from intended use and capture source. If multiple destinations/aspect ratios are needed, compose for safe reframing at storyboard time; do not rely on blind center crop for text, faces, UI, or critical information. Maintain a delivery matrix for widescreen, vertical, square, embedded/site, or archival outputs as applicable. Confirm current platform specifications at export time and keep the high-quality master separate from compressed delivery encodes.

#### MEDIA-03.09 — Keep visual/audio components accessible and localized

Define caption font, size, placement, line length, speaker labels, timing, sound cues, and contrast so captions do not cover essential UI or graphics. Supply human-reviewed caption files and transcript; record translation ownership and terminology glossary for localized versions. Avoid embedding essential text only in the image; localize graphics or supply equivalent transcript/description. Validate each language’s fonts, line breaks, reading speed, names, and timing after export.

#### MEDIA-03.10 — Version and evolve the design system safely

Give tokens, templates, project files, fonts, plugins, and component packages version/owner/license metadata. A change to identity, template, font, animation, or caption convention MUST assess impact on in-progress and published media, compatibility of source project files, and update cost. Keep deprecated templates readable for active projects or define a migration path. Do not silently overwrite reusable assets used by released work.

### MEDIA-04 — Production code, assets, and website integration

*Domain:* [media-production](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/README.md) · *Rule:* [04-production-code-and-web-integration.md](../../torviello-enforcer/media-production/rules/04-production-code-and-web-integration.md)

**Control objective.** Treat video creation automation, large assets, and website playback as maintained product work with clear boundaries, reproducible outputs, quality gates, accessibility, privacy, and operational ownership.

#### MEDIA-04.01 — Select tools by production needs and lifecycle cost

Before choosing an editor/render framework or automation, compare the actual work: manual editing versus data-driven/programmatic scenes; number of repeated variants/locales; timeline/audio requirements; collaboration/review; preview speed; deterministic rendering; format/codec support; accessibility sidecars; platform availability; licenses; security/update posture; hardware/GPU needs; offline use; CI duration/cost; and skill/maintenance burden. Keep creative editing tools where they provide needed timing and judgment; use code-driven rendering when reusable data/templates and reproducible variants justify it. Do not adopt a framework or AI media tool solely because it is available or popular. Record decision owner, tested prototype, alternatives, limitations, and reconsideration triggers.

#### MEDIA-04.02 — Give media production a separate monorepo boundary

When production workflows are maintained in this repository, use a dedicated `media/` product area with its own `README.md` and `AGENTS.md`, toolchain/lockfiles, scripts, tests, asset policy, build instructions, and ownership. It MUST NOT be a dependency of the inference engine’s build, runtime, install, or release profiles. Define explicit interfaces to shared brand assets or website packages; avoid cross-area private imports and toolchain coupling. The `media/` owner MUST maintain a supported build/render entry point and document required licenses, hardware, and external inputs.

#### MEDIA-04.03 — Organize work products for discoverability and safe revision

Use a documented layout equivalent to: `brand/` for approved style guide/tokens; `templates/` for content-type templates and reusable scene components; `projects/<video-id>/` for brief, script, storyboard, claims, rights manifest, and edit/render config; `assets/` for approved shared source media; `automation/` for render, QC, and packaging code; and `releases/<video-id>/<version>/` or an approved artifact store for release manifests and outputs. Large binaries MUST use an approved large-file or artifact store with stable IDs/checksums and access controls rather than silently bloating ordinary source history. Keep project-specific raw footage segregated and access-limited.

#### MEDIA-04.04 — Represent automated scenes as typed, validated inputs

For programmatic video, model scenes, durations, assets, narration, captions, transitions, and output profiles in a validated schema. Give each scene a stable identifier and explicit required/optional assets, timing, locale, and accessibility text. Fail early with actionable diagnostics for missing media, unsupported dimensions, unresolved fonts, invalid time ranges, unlicensed inputs, missing captions, or contradictory project settings. Avoid arbitrary code evaluation or unchecked paths in user-editable project manifests; treat asset paths and project input as untrusted data. Keep rendering deterministic where the toolchain permits and disclose nondeterministic codecs/metadata.

#### MEDIA-04.05 — Pin and inventory production dependencies

Record editor/render-tool versions, plugins, fonts, codecs, templates, scripts, model/AI services if used, and licenses. Pin automated build dependencies and verify downloaded tools/assets; store required binaries in the approved cache/mirror for offline production when offline repeatability is a project requirement. Identify end-of-life/deprecated formats and test migration. Do not promise bit-identical output when codecs, hardware encoders, timestamps, or external services prevent it; define the reproducibility level and verify content/metadata checksums appropriately.

#### MEDIA-04.06 — Build a layered validation pipeline

A production pipeline MUST support separate fast checks and full render/QC. At minimum, validate: project manifest/schema; required sources and rights metadata; broken/missing media and font dependencies; safe paths and secrets; script/claims references; caption and transcript existence and timing; render dimensions/duration/frame rate/codec/color/audio profile; audio/video synchronization; loudness/peak limits for chosen target; black/silent/frozen frames where relevant; output integrity; and playback. Add visual snapshot or representative frame review only as supporting evidence; it cannot replace watching the complete final video. Store machine-readable QC report linked to source revision and render artifact.

#### MEDIA-04.07 — Make builds and renders resource-bounded and resumable

Specify expected render time/storage/GPU/CPU and maximum source dimensions/duration per profile. Provide progress, cancellation, safe temporary-file cleanup, disk-space checks, retry behavior, and recovery from interrupted render without corrupting approved outputs. Bound parallel encodes to available resources. A release render MUST be distinguishable from a preview and MUST NOT overwrite an approved artifact without a new version and review.

#### MEDIA-04.08 — Define explicit delivery profiles and manifests

Maintain versioned profiles for intended destinations rather than one universal export. Each profile defines container, video/audio codecs, dimensions/aspect ratio, frame rate, color space/HDR policy, bitrate or quality mode, audio layout/loudness target, captions, thumbnail, metadata, and validation commands. Record profile version, source revision, toolchain versions, output checksum, and destination requirements in a release manifest. Recheck external destination specifications at release time; keep archived master settings separate from delivery transcodes.

#### MEDIA-04.09 — Integrate video into websites as an accessible, privacy-aware component

A website video component MUST include a poster/thumbnail, descriptive title, playback controls, caption tracks, transcript or equivalent text path, accessible keyboard/focus behavior, responsive sizing, fallback/error behavior, and no autoplay with sound. Provide a local/first-party player or click-to-load consent pattern when third-party embeds would set cookies, load trackers, or contact external domains before user interaction. Lazy-load noncritical media, reserve layout space to prevent layout shift, and do not make video the sole means to understand the page or complete a task. Record supported formats, caption languages, privacy behavior, and fallback.

#### MEDIA-04.10 — Release and operate the production system safely

Deploy production tooling and website changes through their owning release controls. Restrict credentials, signing keys, publication tokens, and private raw media to least privilege; use secret stores rather than project config. Publish only approved release manifests. Monitor render failures, storage saturation, artifact integrity, player failures, accessibility reports, and broken links at a level proportionate to use. Define rollback for website/player releases, disable/revoke publication credentials on compromise, and provide a manual fallback procedure if the render or hosting pipeline is unavailable.

#### MEDIA-04.11 — Test behavior at unit, integration, and release seams

Test scene/schema parsing and timeline calculations as unit tests; test asset resolution, caption association, and render invocation as integration tests; and test representative full renders and playback as release checks. Include malformed manifests, missing assets/fonts, unsupported profile, cancellation, disk exhaustion, encoder/tool failure, caption mismatch, and privacy/embed behavior as applicable. Use tiny synthetic fixtures in CI; reserve full-quality renders for scheduled or release workflows. Document hardware and resource prerequisites; report skipped GPU or platform checks honestly.

### PDL-01 — Problem discovery and evidence

*Domain:* [product-discovery-and-delivery](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/README.md) · *Rule:* [01-problem-discovery-and-evidence.md](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/rules/01-problem-discovery-and-evidence.md)

**Control objective.** Ensure that work begins with a sufficiently supported account of the problem, affected people and systems, desired outcome, uncertainty, and decision authority. Prevent a proposed implementation from being mistaken for validated need.

#### PDL-01.01 — Preserve provenance and distinguish evidence types

The work owner MUST record where a need came from, when and under what conditions it was observed, who is affected, and who is authorized to decide. Records MUST distinguish direct observation or measured evidence from reported experience, interpretation, and assumption. Preserve links, versions, samples, reproduction steps, and limitations needed to revisit a material finding.

#### PDL-01.02 — Describe current state and desired outcome

Before selecting a solution, the owner MUST state the current workflow or system behavior, the user or operational problem, affected actors and contexts, consequence and frequency where known, desired outcome, measurable signal, scope, and explicit non-goals. State how the problem framing could be disproved or materially changed.

#### PDL-01.03 — Select a proportionate discovery method

Choose methods based on the uncertainty and impact being resolved. Options include code/configuration inspection, production telemetry, support and incident records, interviews, contextual observation, usability/accessibility review, stakeholder workshops, market or platform research, prototypes, and controlled experiments. Record why the evidence is relevant, sampling limits, selection bias, recency, and confidence. Do not treat popularity, one anecdote, or a model-generated claim as proof without corroboration proportionate to risk.

#### PDL-01.04 — Protect research participants and sensitive evidence

Before collecting personal or confidential information, determine purpose, lawful/approved basis where applicable, consent or notice needs, minimization, access, retention, redaction, and deletion. Do not place secrets, credentials, personal data, vulnerability details, or commercially sensitive material in public specifications or broad-access planning records. Escalate jurisdiction-dependent obligations to the authorized owner before collection or publication.

#### PDL-01.05 — Map impact and constraints across affected groups

Identify direct and indirect users, operators, maintainers, administrators, contributors, support staff, and external parties whose workflows or risks change. Consider accessibility, language, skills, network/offline conditions, supported hardware/OS, failure consequences, misuse, and groups excluded by the proposed direction. Record material benefits, burdens, and mitigations; do not assume a single “typical user.”

#### PDL-01.06 — Keep uncertainty and unresolved decisions actionable

Maintain assumptions, unknowns, constraints, dependencies, risks, and open decisions with owner, evidence or approval needed, impact if unresolved, target/review trigger, and status. A release-critical unknown MUST block commitment or receive an explicit, authorized risk decision with scope, mitigations, and expiry. Do not silently turn uncertainty into a default product promise.

#### PDL-01.07 — Close discovery with a decision

Conclude each material discovery effort with a recorded decision to proceed, investigate further, change framing, defer, or stop. Cite the evidence and confidence, affected requirement IDs, unresolved matters, and next owner. Preserve negative results and rejected hypotheses so later teams do not repeat work or present disproven assumptions as facts.

### PDL-03 — Solution alternatives and experimentation

*Domain:* [product-discovery-and-delivery](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/README.md) · *Rule:* [03-solution-alternatives-and-experimentation.md](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/rules/03-solution-alternatives-and-experimentation.md)

**Control objective.** Select solutions through explicit, evidence-based decisions and use bounded investigations to reduce uncertainty without confusing experimental artifacts with supported product behavior.

#### PDL-03.01 — Compare feasible alternatives against declared criteria

For each material choice, compare at least the proposed option, a credible alternative, and doing nothing or deferring where feasible. Criteria MUST derive from approved requirements and include lifecycle cost, user impact, risks, compatibility, performance/resource behavior, operational burden, security/privacy, reversibility, and exit options as relevant. Record evidence, uncertainty, tradeoffs, rejected options, and why the selected option best satisfies the criteria.

#### PDL-03.02 — Record decision authority and reconsideration triggers

A decision record MUST identify the accountable authority, consulted roles, affected requirements, scope, date/revision, assumptions, constraints, consequences, and conditions that require review or reversal. Agents and implementers MUST NOT treat a proposal, brainstorm, or unapproved decision as authorization to commit to an irreversible or externally visible choice.

#### PDL-03.03 — Define experiments before running them

A prototype, proof of concept, benchmark, pilot, user study, or other experiment MUST state the question or hypothesis, method, target context, sample/workload, success and stop criteria, data/safety constraints, owner, time/cost bound, and intended decision. Do not expand an experiment into production use without the applicable engineering, security, support, release, and operations gates.

#### PDL-03.04 — Ensure experiments produce interpretable evidence

Record the environment, revision, configuration, inputs, controls/baseline, repetitions or sample limitations, observed results, uncertainty, anomalies, and deviations from protocol. Protect participants and production systems; use synthetic or minimized data when it can answer the question. A benchmark MUST follow the performance rules and MUST NOT generalize beyond its hardware, model, workload, and measurement limits.

#### PDL-03.05 — Close experiments with an explicit outcome

Conclude with adopt, reject, iterate, or inconclusive; map evidence to the predefined criteria and document limitations, follow-up questions, owner, and resulting requirement/decision changes. Negative results MUST be retained. Do not cherry-pick successful runs or redefine success after observing results without disclosing the change and rerunning or qualifying conclusions.

#### PDL-03.06 — Bound prototype exposure and remove or promote artifacts deliberately

Label prototypes and temporary tools with purpose, owner, data classification, intended audience, expiry, known gaps, and whether they MAY be deployed. Before promotion, review architecture, code quality, tests, accessibility, security, privacy, compatibility, licensing, documentation, support, monitoring, and recovery. Otherwise remove or archive safely, including associated data and credentials.

### PDL-05 — Acceptance, release, learning, and retirement

*Domain:* [product-discovery-and-delivery](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/README.md) · *Rule:* [05-acceptance-release-learning-and-retirement.md](../../torviello-enforcer/product-discovery-and-delivery/rules/05-acceptance-release-learning-and-retirement.md)

**Control objective.** Demonstrate that the delivered change meets approved outcomes, make a controlled release decision, observe actual results, and safely change or retire capabilities when evidence warrants it.

#### PDL-05.01 — Verify requirements and validate the intended outcome

Before acceptance, map each applicable requirement to implementation evidence and a check appropriate to its risk. Verification asks whether the specified behavior was built; validation asks whether evidence supports that it solves the intended problem for affected users/operators. Record both where they differ. Include expected, boundary, failure, compatibility, security, accessibility, and recovery evidence as applicable.

#### PDL-05.02 — Make acceptance authority and residual limitations explicit

The designated owner MUST decide accept, reject, conditionally accept, or defer against predeclared criteria. Record tested revision/profile, evidence, failed or unavailable checks, known limitations, residual risks, approved exceptions, migration and rollback readiness. Conditional acceptance MUST identify scope, conditions, owner, expiry, and prohibited use. Agents MAY report evidence but MUST NOT invent delegated approval.

#### PDL-05.03 — Apply domain release gates before exposure

A release or staged exposure MUST satisfy applicable release, security, license, verification, portability, operations, documentation, support, and stakeholder-communication controls. Define rollout cohort/criteria, monitoring, stop/rollback trigger, responsible operator, and user impact. For public launch or irreversible data change, obtain the explicitly authorized decision before exposure.

#### PDL-05.04 — Observe outcomes and act on feedback

For a material change, define post-release signals tied to intended outcomes, safety/security/reliability constraints, and user/operator experience. Set an owner and review point proportionate to risk. Review telemetry, support requests, complaints, incidents, accessibility feedback, regressions, and cost within their privacy and retention limits. Decide to continue, improve, constrain, roll back, or retire; record reasons and resulting work.

#### PDL-05.05 — Maintain traceability and communicate material changes

Update the requirement disposition and links to design, code/change, verification, release, and operational evidence. Communicate behavior, compatibility, migration, support, and known limitations to affected audiences through approved channels. Keep public claims consistent with tested profiles and evidence; coordinate incident or safety communications under applicable controls.

#### PDL-05.06 — Manage changes after acceptance

A change to an accepted requirement, interface, supported profile, data behavior, risk treatment, or acceptance threshold MUST receive impact analysis, owner approval, updated baseline, and re-verification of affected claims. Preserve history so reviewers can determine which revision was approved and why.

#### PDL-05.07 — Retire capabilities and work products safely

Before retirement, identify affected users, clients, operators, integrations, data, models, credentials, dependencies, published artifacts, and contractual commitments. Define notice and transition period, replacement or export path, migration, backup/deletion schedule, access revocation, support end date, rollback limits, and evidence retention. Verify that shutdown removes exposed services and secrets and that retained records meet policy. For an immediate security or safety shutdown, document the emergency rationale and follow incident controls.

#### PDL-05.08 — Feed incidents and failures back into the lifecycle

Material failure, near miss, support pattern, or unmet requirement MUST produce a blameless evidence-based review proportionate to impact. Identify contributing system/process conditions, affected requirements and controls, containment, corrective action owner, due/review trigger, and how effectiveness will be verified. Update rules, specifications, tests, plans, or training when findings show a systemic gap; do not close corrective action solely because a ticket was filed.

### 5.2 Assessment determination

- **CHG-06:** Pass when work is traceable, appropriately planned, verified, independently reviewed where required, accepted by authority, handed off, and closed with evidence. Fail when a transition occurs without its exit evidence, status misrepresents reality, user work is lost, or risk/ownership disappears. Not applicable requires a bounded work class and rationale.
- **MEDIA-01:** Pass when the released artifact matches an approved brief, claims are supported, rights and privacy are cleared, accessibility and technical checks pass, and source/ownership handoff is complete. Fail when any of these cannot be evidenced or the artifact materially misleads. Not applicable requires the bounded internal-use case and deletion controls.
- **MEDIA-02:** Pass when controlled publication destinations are inventoried, accessible alternatives and metadata remain accurate, owner and response paths work, and retention/retirement is evidenced. Fail when owners cannot locate copies or withdraw unsafe content, or when source assets/rights are ungoverned. Not applicable requires a justification that no publication occurred and records are retained or deleted under policy.
- **MEDIA-03:** Pass when design choices are purpose-driven, consistent with an approved or clearly provisional system, accessible in final outputs, adaptable to required destinations, and reproducible from versioned assets. Fail when style or component choices cannot be found/reused, make content inaccessible, misstate evidence, or lack rights/provenance. Not applicable requires the production scope and rationale.
- **MEDIA-04:** Pass when production tools and assets are isolated, understandable, validated, reproducible to the stated level, resource-bounded, and integrated accessibly and privately. Fail when source/tool provenance is missing, release output cannot be traced, engine builds depend on media tooling, critical failures go undetected, or website playback excludes users or violates privacy expectations. Not applicable requires that no code/tool/site integration is used and an owner documents the manual control path.
- **PDL-01:** Pass when applicable MUST clauses have current evidence and material unresolved matters have an owner and disposition. Fail when a solution is treated as a need, material groups or impacts are omitted, sensitive evidence is mishandled, or a critical unknown is hidden. Not applicable requires the work scope and rationale.
- **PDL-03:** Pass when material choices are reasoned and authorized, experiments are bounded and reproducible enough for their claims, and artifacts have a deliberate disposition. Fail for hidden uncertainty, unsafe experiment exposure, post-hoc success criteria, or undocumented production promotion.
- **PDL-05:** Pass when acceptance, release, operational learning, and retirement decisions are evidence-based, authorized, and traceable. Fail when release gates are bypassed, evidence cannot support claims, or users/data/dependencies are abandoned without a controlled transition. Not applicable requires rationale.

- **CHG-06:** A MUST exception requires the accountable delivery/product/system owner and relevant risk authority. Emergency exceptions follow the emergency-change control. No exception may authorize loss or concealment of user changes or mandatory legal/contractual violations.
- **MEDIA-01:** A MUST exception requires the accountable product/marketing owner and relevant rights, privacy, security, accessibility, or legal authority. Record the artifact/scene, audience, residual risk, mitigation, owner, and expiry. Do not publish while mandatory rights, consent, or legal questions remain unresolved.
- **MEDIA-02:** A MUST exception requires the accountable communications/product owner and relevant legal, rights, privacy, security, or accessibility authority. - **MEDIA-04:** A MUST exception requires the media engineering owner plus affected website, security/privacy, release, accessibility, or licensing authority. Record risk, limited scope, compensating controls, remediation owner, and expiry/review date. Do not waive mandatory legal or contractual obligations.

- **PDL-05:** A MUST exception requires accountable product/system authority plus applicable release, security, privacy, legal, operations, or safety authority. Record scope, users/profiles affected, residual risk, mitigation, owner, expiry, and communication. An exception cannot waive mandatory legal or contractual duties.

## 8. Prohibited conditions

The following conditions are explicitly prohibited by the mapped controls. Any one of them is sufficient to fail the requirement.

- [CHG-06] Editing before checking whether the target branch/worktree contains user changes.
- [CHG-06] Treating a plan, code completion, merge, or passing unit tests as acceptance or production release by itself.
- [CHG-06] Hiding failed, skipped, flaky, or unavailable checks; changing acceptance after the result without disclosure.
- [CHG-06] Closing work with unowned risk, undocumented migration/release effects, or missing durable decisions.
- [MEDIA-01] Publishing an unreviewed take with credentials, personal/customer data, confidential material, or unsupported claims.
- [MEDIA-01] Relying only on auto-generated captions for release.
- [MEDIA-01] Using music, images, fonts, logos, people, or customer material without verified rights/permission.
- [MEDIA-01] Presenting a mockup, edited result, prototype, benchmark, or roadmap as generally available product behavior.
- [MEDIA-01] Publishing a video whose source owner, update contact, or takedown path is unknown.
- [MEDIA-02] Publishing from an uncontrolled personal account with no organizational owner or recovery route.
- [MEDIA-02] Treating an edit or re-upload as complete without refreshing captions, claims review, metadata, and dependent embeds.
- [MEDIA-02] Keeping raw footage indefinitely without purpose, access rules, or retention decision.
- [MEDIA-02] Claiming complete takedown when copies outside project control cannot be confirmed removed.
- [MEDIA-03] Treating decorative motion, music, stock footage, or a long branded intro as mandatory components.
- [MEDIA-03] Creating per-video typography, colors, transitions, lower thirds, or audio conventions that conflict with the approved system without an approved exception.
- [MEDIA-03] Reusing components that obscure product evidence, cover captions, misrepresent UI, or introduce unreadable text on small screens.
- [MEDIA-03] Building templates around inaccessible baked-in text or unlicensed fonts/plugins/assets.
- [MEDIA-04] Coupling video rendering or marketing site dependencies into the inference engine’s build/runtime by convenience.
- [MEDIA-04] Publishing artifacts with no source revision, delivery profile, checksum, rights record, or QC outcome.
- [MEDIA-04] Passing an automated QC report as a substitute for human review of narrative, accuracy, accessibility, and complete playback.
- [MEDIA-04] Loading third-party players or trackers before required consent/privacy behavior is implemented.
- [MEDIA-04] Embedding secrets or publication credentials in source, project files, URLs, or logs.
- [PDL-01] Starting consequential work solely from an unexamined solution request.
- [PDL-01] Presenting an assumption, estimate, prototype, or anecdote as measured fact.
- [PDL-01] Collecting participant or production data without defined purpose and handling controls.
- [PDL-01] Committing to a user, platform, legal, or service promise whose owner or evidence is unknown.
- [PDL-03] Selecting a material design solely from familiarity, preference, or sunk cost.
- [PDL-03] Using a prototype, pilot, or benchmark as evidence of production readiness without applicable gates.
- [PDL-03] Running experiments with uncontrolled sensitive data or unbounded impact on users/services.
- [PDL-03] Treating inconclusive evidence as confirmation.
- [PDL-05] Acceptance based solely on implementation completion or author assertion.
- [PDL-05] Release with unknown critical failures or risks lacking authorized disposition.
- [PDL-05] Public claims beyond tested and supported behavior.
- [PDL-05] Retiring a service or data capability without resolving user transition, access, and retention obligations.
