# ED-055 — Every implementation change and test shall be traceable to a contract, specification, or business rule, and every critical requirement shall have implementation and verification evidence

**Requirement ID:** ED-055  
**Priority:** MUST  
**Source:** [engineering-and-delivery.md](../engineering-and-delivery.md) — Development workflow (DDD → SDD → CDD → TDD → EDD)  
**Status:** Active requirement; targeted contract and runtime evidence is recorded in the assurance register, but acceptance is not claimed.
**Rule controls:** GOV-06, GOV-07, GOV-08, GOV-09, GOV-10  
**Acceptance checks:** None recorded  
**Open decisions:** None recorded

## 1. Normative requirement

Every implementation change and test shall be traceable to a contract, specification, or business rule, and every critical requirement shall have implementation and verification evidence.

## 2. Why this requirement exists

This requirement is part of **Development workflow (DDD → SDD → CDD → TDD → EDD)** in [engineering-and-delivery.md](../engineering-and-delivery.md). It is traced to 5 rule control(s): GOV-06, GOV-07, GOV-08, GOV-09, GOV-10. Those controls state the following purpose:

- **GOV-06 — Domain-Driven Design (DDD):** Behavior-changing work MUST start from the business domain: the concepts that must exist, their invariants, their bounded contexts, and their owners. DDD establishes the meaning that specifications, contracts, tests, and evaluations trace back to. This control is additive to the existing domain controls and does not replace them.
- **GOV-07 — Specification-Driven Development (SDD):** Specification-Driven Development states exactly what the system must do before implementation. A specification MUST turn domain rules into testable use cases, functional requirements with stable identifiers, edge cases, and observable acceptance criteria so that implementation, contracts, tests, and evaluation share one unambiguous target.
- **GOV-08 — Contract-Driven Development (CDD):** Contract-Driven Development fixes how components interact. Interfaces, inputs, outputs, errors, events, and versioning MUST be defined in machine-readable contracts that consumers and providers agree on, and contract changes MUST be versioned and verified by contract tests rather than prose.
- **GOV-09 — Test-Driven Development (TDD):** Test-Driven Development verifies correctness while implementation proceeds. Approved behaviors and edge cases MUST be driven by failing tests through the RED → GREEN → REFACTOR cycle, with unit, contract, and integration tests kept in their categories. A passing run on a weakened, skipped, or deleted test MUST NOT count as verification.
- **GOV-10 — Evaluation-Driven Development (EDD):** Evaluation-Driven Development decides whether the complete solution is acceptable. Before release, the project MUST evaluate functionality, contract conformance, authorization, performance, and release-blocking defects against pre-established thresholds, and MUST record dated metrics and a release decision. Evaluation is broader than rerunning unit tests.

Acceptance is not satisfied by citing these controls. The implementation must demonstrate the observable result in section 6, and the mapped rule clauses in section 5 define what must hold at the enforcement boundary.

## 3. Priority and interpretation

This requirement has **MUST** priority under the repository's requirement conventions. A MUST requirement is needed for the stated product direction or a safe release. A SHOULD requirement is expected unless a documented tradeoff justifies deferral. A COULD requirement is a candidate capability that requires product or technical validation. Acceptance describes observable evidence; it does not mandate a specific implementation unless the requirement says so.

## 4. Scope and applicability

This requirement belongs to **Development workflow (DDD → SDD → CDD → TDD → EDD)** in [engineering-and-delivery.md](../engineering-and-delivery.md). It applies to the product area named by that source section and to every implementation, interface, release artifact, document, or operation that claims to satisfy it.

The mapped controls add the following applicability boundaries:

- **GOV-06:** This control applies to every feature, remediation, and agent-assisted change that introduces or changes business behavior, business rules, or domain ownership. A purely mechanical change that does not affect domain meaning MAY be recorded as not applicable with a decision owner. Product-specific choices remain governed by approved requirements and decision records.
- **GOV-07:** This control applies to every feature, remediation, and agent-assisted change that alters observable behavior or acceptance criteria. A change with no observable behavior effect MAY record SDD as not applicable with a decision owner. Product-specific choices remain governed by approved requirements and decision records.
- **GOV-08:** This control applies to every change that adds or modifies an interface, API endpoint, event, message, schema, or integration boundary. A change with no external or cross-component interaction MAY record CDD as not applicable with a decision owner. Product-specific choices remain governed by approved requirements and decision records.
- **GOV-09:** This control applies to every change that adds or modifies executable behavior, interfaces, or test suites. Documentation-only or non-executable artifact changes MAY record TDD as not applicable with a decision owner. Product-specific choices remain governed by approved requirements and decision records.
- **GOV-10:** This control applies to every release candidate and every change that claims completion of a requirement or feature. A change that is not part of a release candidate MAY record EDD as not applicable with a decision owner. Product-specific choices remain governed by approved requirements and decision records.

The source requirement table remains authoritative if this view and the source disagree.

## 5. Detailed obligations

Each subsection below is the complete normative content of a mapped control. A requirement is not satisfied by selecting one convenient clause; every applicable clause must hold, including its negative and boundary behavior.

### GOV-06 — Domain-Driven Design (DDD)

*Domain:* [governance](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/README.md) · *Rule:* [06-domain-driven-design.md](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/rules/06-domain-driven-design.md)

**Control objective.** Behavior-changing work MUST start from the business domain: the concepts that must exist, their invariants, their bounded contexts, and their owners. DDD establishes the meaning that specifications, contracts, tests, and evaluations trace back to. This control is additive to the existing domain controls and does not replace them.

#### GOV-06.01 — Work that changes behavior MUST begin with a domain model…

**Requirement:** Work that changes behavior MUST begin with a domain model identifying the bounded context, business concepts, invariants, ownership, and the events or state transitions that matter. The model MUST be established before specifications and implementation, and MAY be revised when later stages expose a gap.

#### GOV-06.02 — The repository MUST contain maintained domain artifacts…

**Requirement:** The repository MUST contain maintained domain artifacts that capture the domain model, ubiquitous language or glossary, business rules and invariants, bounded contexts, and ownership. These artifacts MUST live in a discoverable location, and physical separation from code is OPTIONAL; discoverability is REQUIRED.

#### GOV-06.03 — A business concept or invariant MUST NOT exist only in…

**Requirement:** A business concept or invariant MUST NOT exist only in infrastructure names, database tables, or scattered code. Each business rule MUST be stated in a domain artifact and referenced by the specification that realizes it.

#### GOV-06.04 — Every domain rule MUST have a stable identifier and MUST be…

**Requirement:** Every domain rule MUST have a stable identifier and MUST be traceable forward to its specification, contract, tests, and evaluation evidence. A domain rule with no forward reference MUST be treated as unimplemented or unverified, not as satisfied.

#### GOV-06.05 — A change to the domain model MUST update affected…

**Requirement:** A change to the domain model MUST update the affected specifications, contracts, tests, and evaluations in the same change, or record why an affected artifact is unchanged. Silent domain drift between the model and the implementation is prohibited.

### GOV-07 — Specification-Driven Development (SDD)

*Domain:* [governance](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/README.md) · *Rule:* [07-specification-driven-development.md](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/rules/07-specification-driven-development.md)

**Control objective.** Specification-Driven Development states exactly what the system must do before implementation. A specification MUST turn domain rules into testable use cases, functional requirements with stable identifiers, edge cases, and observable acceptance criteria so that implementation, contracts, tests, and evaluation share one unambiguous target.

#### GOV-07.01 — A feature or behavior change MUST have a specification before…

**Requirement:** A feature or behavior change MUST have a specification before implementation that states its use cases, functional requirements with stable identifiers, edge cases, and acceptance criteria. The specification MUST identify the domain rules it realizes.

#### GOV-07.02 — Acceptance criteria MUST be observable, testable, and…

**Requirement:** Acceptance criteria MUST be observable, testable, and unambiguous. A specification MUST state the given conditions, the action, and the expected outcome, or an equivalent executable scenario form. Subjective qualities MUST be expressed as measurable criteria.

#### GOV-07.03 — Every functional requirement MUST have a stable identifier that…

**Requirement:** Every functional requirement MUST have a stable identifier that resolves in the repository, and each identifier MUST be referenced by the contract, tests, or evaluation evidence that verifies it. A dangling or reused identifier is a defect.

#### GOV-07.04 — Acceptance checks MUST be defined before implementation…

**Requirement:** Acceptance checks MUST be defined before implementation, and the plan MUST map each requirement identifier to the check, expected outcome, and whether the check runs automatically or manually. A specification without verification intent MUST NOT be treated as approved.

#### GOV-07.05 — An approved specification MUST NOT be rewritten, narrowed, or…

**Requirement:** An approved specification MUST NOT be rewritten, narrowed, or silently reinterpreted to make an implementation pass. A necessary change to approved behavior REQUIRES human authority and MUST be recorded as a specification change with the decision owner and the affected requirements.

#### GOV-07.06 — Specifications MUST remain discoverable and consistent with…

**Requirement:** Specifications MUST remain discoverable and consistent with the domain model, contracts, and delivered behavior. Contradictions between a specification and the shipped behavior MUST be reported as nonconformance rather than resolved by editing the specification alone.

### GOV-08 — Contract-Driven Development (CDD)

*Domain:* [governance](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/README.md) · *Rule:* [08-contract-driven-development.md](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/rules/08-contract-driven-development.md)

**Control objective.** Contract-Driven Development fixes how components interact. Interfaces, inputs, outputs, errors, events, and versioning MUST be defined in machine-readable contracts that consumers and providers agree on, and contract changes MUST be versioned and verified by contract tests rather than prose.

#### GOV-08.01 — Every interface and event between components MUST be defined…

**Requirement:** Every interface and event between components MUST be defined in a machine-readable contract covering endpoints or channels, inputs, outputs, error shapes, and event payloads. OpenAPI, JSON Schema, AsyncAPI, or an equivalent schema format MUST be used; prose alone MUST NOT be the contract.

#### GOV-08.02 — Consumers and providers MUST agree on the contract, and the…

**Requirement:** Consumers and providers MUST agree on the contract, and the repository MUST contain contract tests that verify both sides against it. A contract without a failing-then-passing contract test MUST NOT be treated as verified.

#### GOV-08.03 — Contract changes MUST be versioned and MUST state…

**Requirement:** Contract changes MUST be versioned and MUST state compatibility impact. A breaking change MUST have a migration or deprecation path, and the affected consumers MUST be identified before the change is released.

#### GOV-08.04 — Contracts MUST be linted or validated by tooling as part of…

**Requirement:** Contracts MUST be linted or validated by tooling as part of the required checks, and validation failures MUST block the change. A schema that is not parsed by any check is unverified documentation.

#### GOV-08.05 — Every contract element used by implementation or tests MUST be…

**Requirement:** Every contract element used by implementation or tests MUST be traceable to a specification and requirement identifier, and the contract MUST be referenced by the tests that exercise it. An orphan endpoint or event is a defect.

#### GOV-08.06 — An approved contract MUST NOT be altered to match an…

**Requirement:** An approved contract MUST NOT be altered to match an implementation that does not satisfy it. Contract changes REQUIRE human authority and MUST be recorded as a contract decision with consumer impact and versioning.

#### GOV-08.07 — Contracts MUST precede contract tests and provider implementation…

**Requirement:** For every interface change, the machine-readable contract MUST be authored, reviewed, and approved before contract tests are written, and contract tests MUST be written and observed failing before provider implementation. A test or implementation that introduces or changes an undecided interface MUST be rejected until the contract is updated and approved. The mandatory order is contract → failing test → implementation.

#### GOV-08.08 — Every operation declared in an HTTP contract MUST be exercised…

**Requirement:** Every operation declared in an HTTP contract MUST be exercised against the running provider by a contract integration test that uses the operation's declared method and path. The test MUST fail when the operation is unrouted, returns a status or media type not declared for that operation, or violates the contract's required headers, cache policy, body fields, or allowed error codes. Operations declared reserved MUST return the declared `501` problem response; operations not declared reserved MUST NOT return `501`.

### GOV-09 — Test-Driven Development (TDD)

*Domain:* [governance](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/README.md) · *Rule:* [09-test-driven-development.md](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/rules/09-test-driven-development.md)

**Control objective.** Test-Driven Development verifies correctness while implementation proceeds. Approved behaviors and edge cases MUST be driven by failing tests through the RED → GREEN → REFACTOR cycle, with unit, contract, and integration tests kept in their categories. A passing run on a weakened, skipped, or deleted test MUST NOT count as verification.

#### GOV-09.01 — Each approved behavior and edge case MUST be driven by a test…

**Requirement:** Each approved behavior and edge case MUST be driven by a test that fails for the right reason before the implementation exists (RED), then passes after the minimal implementation (GREEN), and remains passing through refactoring (REFACTOR). The cycle MAY repeat per behavior, but the failing-first evidence MUST exist.

#### GOV-09.02 — Unit, contract, integration, and end-to-end tests MUST be kept…

**Requirement:** Unit, contract, integration, and end-to-end tests MUST be kept in their dedicated categories and MUST be independently selectable with documented prerequisites. A monolith test command that cannot isolate a category is insufficient.

#### GOV-09.03 — Tests MUST be deterministic and MUST NOT depend on…

**Requirement:** Tests MUST be deterministic and MUST NOT depend on unbounded sleeps, external networks, shared mutable state, or ordering between tests. Flaky, skipped, or quarantined tests MUST be reported and MUST NOT be counted as passing evidence.

#### GOV-09.04 — Contract and integration behavior MUST be verified by…

**Requirement:** Contract and integration behavior MUST be verified by tests that exercise the approved contract and its error paths, not only the happy path. Model, hardware, or environment-dependent tests MUST state their prerequisites and MUST NOT silently skip on unsupported environments.

#### GOV-09.05 — Tests MUST NOT be weakened, skipped, deleted, or made…

**Requirement:** Tests MUST NOT be weakened, skipped, deleted, or made non-asserting to make a change pass. Such a change REQUIRES human authority and MUST be recorded with the affected requirement and the reason; a green run on a weakened test MUST NOT be reported as verification.

#### GOV-09.06 — Every test MUST be traceable to a requirement, specification, or…

**Requirement:** Every test MUST be traceable to a requirement, specification, or contract, and the repository MUST record the command and result used to produce the passing evidence. Coverage claims MUST name the tests and the scope they cover.

#### GOV-09.07 — Contract-first ordering MUST precede failing-first implementation…

**Requirement:** For interface changes, failing-first tests MUST be written against the approved contract before implementation begins. Tests MUST NOT be used to discover or retroactively define the contract; a test whose expectations are absent from the contract MUST be rejected until the contract is updated and approved. The mandatory order is contract → failing test → implementation.

### GOV-10 — Evaluation-Driven Development (EDD)

*Domain:* [governance](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/README.md) · *Rule:* [10-evaluation-driven-development.md](../../../torviello-enforcer/governance/rules/10-evaluation-driven-development.md)

**Control objective.** Evaluation-Driven Development decides whether the complete solution is acceptable. Before release, the project MUST evaluate functionality, contract conformance, authorization, performance, and release-blocking defects against pre-established thresholds, and MUST record dated metrics and a release decision. Evaluation is broader than rerunning unit tests.

#### GOV-10.01 — Release thresholds MUST be defined before evaluation begins…

**Requirement:** Release thresholds MUST be defined before evaluation begins and MUST cover functionality, contract verification, authorization, performance, and release-blocking defects. Thresholds MUST be measurable, owned, and recorded in an evaluation plan.

#### GOV-10.02 — Evaluation MUST produce a dated report with the command…

**Requirement:** Evaluation MUST produce a dated report with the command, scope, environment, metrics, dataset or workload, and results for each threshold. It MUST include checks beyond unit tests, such as contract tests, authorization tests, performance measurements, and defect review.

#### GOV-10.03 — Each threshold MUST receive an explicit pass or fail…

**Requirement:** Each threshold MUST receive an explicit pass or fail result. An unmet threshold MUST be reported as a failure and MUST block release until resolved or covered by an approved, time-bounded exception; it MUST NOT be redefined, narrowed, or excluded after the result is known.

#### GOV-10.04 — A named decision owner MUST record the release decision…

**Requirement:** A named decision owner MUST record the release decision against the evaluation report. Automation MAY collect results, but the release gate MUST NOT be marked passed on document presence, partial scope, or an unrecorded verbal decision.

#### GOV-10.05 — Evaluation findings MUST route back to the stage that must…

**Requirement:** Evaluation findings MUST route back to the stage that must change — domain, specification, contract, or implementation — and the return MUST be recorded with the affected clause and requirement. Repeating evaluation after a fix MUST use the same thresholds.

#### GOV-10.06 — An evaluation MUST NOT relax thresholds, remove critical…

**Requirement:** An evaluation MUST NOT relax thresholds, remove critical cases, or redefine success to make a release pass. Such a change REQUIRES human authority and MUST be recorded as a threshold decision with the accountable owner and the risk accepted.

#### GOV-10.07 — Every critical requirement MUST have evaluation evidence…

**Requirement:** Every critical requirement MUST have evaluation evidence that resolves from the requirement to the report and result. Missing or stale evaluation evidence MUST be reported as a failure or an explicit assessment limitation, never as a pass.


### 5.1 Implementation guidance

- **GOV-06:** Keep the domain artifact close to the specifications it feeds. Use stable rule identifiers that also appear in the specification and tests so tooling can verify the forward trace.
- **GOV-07:** Prefer executable scenario files or specification sections with stable identifiers. Keep the identifier on every test and contract so the plan-to-verification mapping is mechanical.
- **GOV-08:** Keep contracts in a discoverable schema location and generate or validate consumer and provider bindings from them. Reference the contract path and version in the tests and specification.
- **GOV-09:** Commit the failing test before the implementation when practical. Keep test names carrying the requirement identifier and the behavior, so coverage and traceability are mechanical.
- **GOV-10:** Keep the evaluation plan under version control and generate the report from the executed checks where possible. Store datasets or workload definitions with the report so results are reproducible.

### 5.2 Assessment determination

- **GOV-06:** The control passes only when the domain model and invariants are documented for the affected context, each affected rule has a stable identifier and resolvable forward trace, and the implementation does not contradict the model. Missing domain artifacts, code-only rules, or broken forward traces are failures. A recorded not-applicable decision with a decision owner is acceptable only when the change has no domain effect.
- **GOV-07:** The control passes only when the specification exists before implementation, criteria are observable and testable, identifiers resolve and are referenced by verification evidence, and no approved behavior was silently changed. Missing criteria, dangling identifiers, or unrecorded specification changes are failures.
- **GOV-08:** The control passes only when machine-readable contracts define the affected interfaces, contract tests verify both sides and pass, validation runs in required checks, and every used contract element traces to a specification and requirement. Prose-only contracts, missing contract tests, unversioned breaking changes, or orphan elements are failures.
- **GOV-09:** The control passes only when approved behaviors have failing-first tests, required categories are independently selectable, tests are deterministic and traceable, and the recorded commands and results show them passing without weakening. Missing failing-first evidence, non-deterministic tests, or weakened tests are failures.
- **GOV-10:** The control passes only when thresholds were pre-established, a dated report covers functionality, contracts, authorization, performance, and release-blocking defects, each threshold has an explicit result, failures block release or have an approved exception, a named owner recorded the decision, and findings are routed back. Missing thresholds, post-hoc threshold changes, partial scope, or absent evidence are failures.

### 5.3 Exceptions and nonconformance

- **GOV-06:** An exception MUST identify the affected clauses, reason, risk, compensating controls, accountable approver, remediation owner, and expiry/review date, and MUST be recorded in the assurance register before it is effective. A change whose domain rule cannot be traced forward is nonconforming and MUST NOT be reported as passing.
- **GOV-07:** An exception MUST identify the affected clauses, reason, risk, compensating controls, accountable approver, remediation owner, and expiry/review date, and MUST be recorded in the assurance register before it is effective. Delivered behavior that contradicts an approved specification without an authorized change is nonconforming and MUST NOT be reported as passing.
- **GOV-08:** An exception MUST identify the affected clauses, reason, risk, compensating controls, accountable approver, remediation owner, and expiry/review date, and MUST be recorded in the assurance register before it is effective. An interface shipped without a verified contract is nonconforming and MUST NOT be reported as passing.
- **GOV-09:** An exception MUST identify the affected clauses, reason, risk, compensating controls, accountable approver, remediation owner, and expiry/review date, and MUST be recorded in the assurance register before it is effective. A change that passes only because a test was weakened is nonconforming and MUST NOT be reported as passing.
- **GOV-10:** An exception MUST identify the affected clauses, the unmet threshold, reason, risk and impacted parties, compensating controls, accountable approver, remediation owner, and expiry/review date, and MUST be recorded in the assurance register before it is effective. An unmet threshold without an approved exception is a release blocker and MUST NOT be reported as a pass.

## 8. Prohibited conditions

The following conditions are explicitly prohibited by the mapped controls. Any one of them is sufficient to fail the requirement.

- [GOV-06] Treating code or database names as the only definition of a business concept.
- [GOV-06] Claiming DDD was performed because a document exists while no rule is traceable to verification.
- [GOV-06] Changing a business invariant in code without updating the domain artifact and its dependent specifications.
- [GOV-06] Creating multiple conflicting glossaries or bounded-context definitions without an owner decision.
- [GOV-07] Implementing behavior before any testable acceptance criteria exist.
- [GOV-07] Using vague criteria such as "works well" or "fast enough" without a measurable threshold.
- [GOV-07] Referring to requirement identifiers that do not resolve in the repository.
- [GOV-07] Rewriting or narrowing an approved specification to match a convenient implementation.
- [GOV-07] Treating a specification document alone as evidence that behavior exists.
- [GOV-08] Describing an interface only in prose, comments, or documentation without a machine-readable schema.
- [GOV-08] Shipping a contract change without contract tests or without a version and compatibility statement.
- [GOV-08] Changing an approved contract to hide an implementation defect.
- [GOV-08] Leaving endpoints or events in the contract that no specification, test, or implementation references.
- [GOV-08] Treating schema file presence as contract verification.
- [GOV-08] Leaving an operation declared in the contract unexercised by a provider integration test.
- [GOV-08] Marking an operation reserved while returning a success response, or marking it implemented while returning `501`.
- [GOV-09] Writing implementation first and adding tests that cannot fail afterward.
- [GOV-09] Deleting, skipping, or narrowing assertions to obtain a green run.
- [GOV-09] Counting a flaky test, a quarantined test, or a skipped test as verification.
- [GOV-09] Running a single undifferentiated test command that cannot select the required category.
- [GOV-09] Claiming verification without recording the command and result that produced it.
- [GOV-10] Defining thresholds after seeing results or moving a threshold to match a failing metric.
- [GOV-10] Treating unit tests alone as a complete evaluation.
- [GOV-10] Reporting a release gate as passed without a dated report and a named decision owner.
- [GOV-10] Removing failing cases, narrowing scope, or redefining success to obtain a pass.
- [GOV-10] Ignoring evaluation findings instead of routing them back to the affected stage.
- [GOV-10] Claiming product conformance from rules text, document presence, or an empty report.
